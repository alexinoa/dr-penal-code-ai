# Source Hierarchy

This file guides retrieval priority. It does not itself determine legal validity; the controlling Dominican legal framework does.

## Hierarchy

1. **Constitution of the Dominican Republic**
2. **Controlling current criminal legislation**, including the current Penal Code and later amendments
3. **Código Procesal Penal**
4. **Special criminal statutes** applicable to the subject matter
5. **Official regulations and implementing norms**
6. **Official jurisprudence**, classified according to court and legal force
7. **Earlier versions and repealed/historical laws** for temporal analysis
8. **Official explanatory materials**
9. **Secondary summaries, indexes, and project guides**

## Amendment rule

A later valid amendment controls over the earlier wording of the same provision. In the current corpus, Ley 44-26 must be checked whenever it modifies Ley 74-25.

## Special-law rule

Do not assume that the Penal Code exhausts a subject. Search the relevant special statute when the issue concerns cybercrime, money laundering, firearms, drugs, children/adolescents, trafficking, migration, public corruption, law-enforcement powers, or another specially regulated field.

## Historical-law rule

Historical law is not "wrong" merely because it is older. It may control conduct committed while it was in force, subject to constitutional principles and rules on favorability. Label historical law clearly.

## Secondary-source rule

Indexes, glossaries, executive summaries, and AI-generated guides are navigation aids only. They must never override the official legal text. If an index conflicts with a statute, follow the statute and flag the index for correction.

## Authenticity rule

When duplicate copies differ, prefer the official promulgated/Gaceta version. OCR or Markdown conversions should preserve article numbering and wording, but possible conversion errors must be verified against the official publication.
