# Research Methodology

## Objective

Produce answers that can be traced to the uploaded Dominican legal authorities and that distinguish current law, historical law, amendments, procedure, and secondary explanation.

## Research sequence

### A. Frame the question

Extract:
- alleged conduct;
- date or date range;
- location;
- status of the persons involved when legally relevant;
- whether there is an active case;
- the specific question the user wants answered.

### B. Search primary sources

Search by:
- legal concept;
- common-language synonym;
- offense name;
- article number if provided;
- key factual terms;
- special-law topic.

Read neighboring articles because definitions, exceptions, aggravating factors, and penalties may be located outside the first matching provision.

### C. Verify amendments

Before treating an article as current:
- search later amendments;
- inspect repeal/replacement clauses;
- inspect effective-date provisions;
- check whether a special statute remains applicable.

For Ley 74-25, check Ley 44-26 whenever the topic falls within a modified provision.

### D. Cross-reference procedure

If the user's question concerns enforcement or an active case, search the Code of Criminal Procedure for:
- type of criminal action;
- jurisdiction/competence;
- investigation powers;
- arrest and detention;
- searches and seizures;
- coercive measures;
- evidence;
- deadlines and prescription;
- appeals;
- execution.

### E. Cross-reference Constitution

Search constitutional rights when the issue affects liberty, privacy, expression, due process, equality, property, dignity, judicial protection, or another fundamental right.

### F. Search special laws

Use special statutes for subject areas they specifically regulate. Do not assume the Penal Code displaced a special statute unless the legal text says so.

### G. Jurisprudence

When official decisions are available, verify the exact decision and relevant holding. Never cite a remembered case number without retrieving it.

### H. Draft from verified material

Separate:
- direct legal text;
- paraphrase/explanation;
- factual application;
- uncertainty or unresolved issue.

## Research-stop rule

If the available corpus does not support a material proposition, do not fill the gap from intuition. State what could not be verified and identify the missing authority.
