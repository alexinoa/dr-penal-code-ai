# Legal Reasoning Engine

This guide defines the analytical workflow for Dominican criminal-law questions. It is a reasoning aid only; official legal texts control.

## Core workflow

1. **Identify the legal issue.** Separate substantive criminal law, criminal procedure, constitutional rights, special-law issues, civil consequences, and evidentiary questions.
2. **Establish the relevant date.** Determine when the alleged conduct occurred and whether later amendments affect the analysis.
3. **Identify jurisdiction and competence.** Confirm territorial, subject-matter, and procedural competence when relevant.
4. **Locate controlling authority.** Start with the Constitution, current Penal Code and amendments, Code of Criminal Procedure, then special statutes and official jurisprudence.
5. **Break the offense into elements.** Identify conduct, result, causation, required mental state, status of victim/offender, aggravating circumstances, and exclusions.
6. **Apply facts cautiously.** Explain which facts support or fail to support each element. Do not convert allegations into findings.
7. **Analyze participation.** Consider authorship, complicity, attempt, corporate liability, and related offenses only when supported by the sources.
8. **Analyze consequences.** Verify imprisonment range, fines, complementary penalties, security measures, restitution, confiscation, and prescription.
9. **Analyze procedure.** Where relevant, identify action type, arrest/search rules, coercive measures, evidence, deadlines, appeals, and execution rules.
10. **Analyze constitutional safeguards.** Consider legality, due process, presumption of innocence, non-self-incrimination, privacy, freedom, equality, proportionality, and access to justice when implicated.
11. **Identify defenses and exceptions.** Only describe defenses grounded in available authority or clearly label them as issues requiring further research.
12. **State uncertainty.** Identify missing facts, conflicting authorities, amendments, OCR problems, or unavailable jurisprudence.
13. **Conclude narrowly.** State what the sources support, what remains uncertain, and what additional facts or authority are needed.

## Temporal analysis

For conduct spanning old and new legislation:

- identify the law in force when the conduct occurred;
- identify later amendments;
- determine whether a transitional provision applies;
- consider non-retroactivity of harsher criminal law;
- consider application of a more favorable later rule where legally permitted;
- distinguish substantive rules from procedural rules, which may have different temporal treatment.

## Conflict resolution

When sources conflict:

1. Prefer Constitution over ordinary legislation.
2. Prefer later valid amendments over earlier text on the same provision.
3. Prefer specific law over general law when legally applicable.
4. Prefer official publication over summaries, guides, or OCR-derived text.
5. Do not silently reconcile uncertain conflicts. State the conflict and identify what must be verified.

## Minimum output standard

A complete legal analysis should make clear:

- the issue;
- the controlling source;
- the relevant article(s);
- the elements;
- the factual application;
- the possible penalty or procedural consequence;
- important exceptions/defenses;
- missing information;
- source citations.
