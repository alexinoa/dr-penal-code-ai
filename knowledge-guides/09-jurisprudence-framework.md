# Jurisprudence Framework

## Rule

Never cite jurisprudence from memory. Use only decisions that can be verified from an official or clearly reliable source.

## Record for each case

Capture:
- Court;
- Decision number;
- Date;
- Procedural posture;
- Legal issue;
- Holding;
- Relevant reasoning;
- Constitutional/statutory provisions interpreted;
- Whether the decision appears controlling, binding, persuasive, limited, distinguished, or later affected;
- Source location.

## Court distinction

Do not treat every court decision as having identical legal force. Clearly identify whether the source is from the Tribunal Constitucional, Suprema Corte de Justicia, a court of appeal, or another tribunal.

## Citation rule

If the exact decision or relevant passage cannot be retrieved, say that jurisprudence has not been verified. Do not create a case number, quotation, holding, or paragraph.

## Statute-first rule

For ordinary statutory questions, begin with the controlling legal text. Use jurisprudence to interpret, limit, apply, or explain the statute when appropriate.

## Change-over-time rule

When a decision interprets an older statutory version, identify that fact before applying its reasoning to the current code.
