# Criminal Offense Framework

For each potentially applicable offense, analyze only the elements supported by the controlling legal text.

## Checklist

- **Protected legal interest:** What interest or right does the provision protect?
- **Conduct:** What act or omission is prohibited?
- **Result:** Is a particular result required?
- **Causation:** Must the conduct cause that result?
- **Mental state:** Intent, knowledge, recklessness/negligence, or another required state.
- **Offender qualifications:** Does the offense require a public official, professional, family relationship, corporate role, or other special status?
- **Victim qualifications:** Does age, vulnerability, relationship, or status matter?
- **Object/instrument:** Weapon, data, controlled substance, public funds, document, etc.
- **Attempt:** Is attempt punishable and under what rule?
- **Participation:** Authorship, complicity, instigation, corporate liability.
- **Aggravating circumstances:** Identify each statutory aggravator separately.
- **Mitigating/exonerating rules:** Include only verified statutory rules.
- **Penalty:** Verify imprisonment, fine, complementary penalties, measures, confiscation, or restitution.
- **Prescription:** Check substantive and procedural sources, including special rules.
- **Related offenses:** Identify alternative or overlapping classifications without double-counting conduct.
- **Applicable special law:** Check whether a special statute supplements or controls.

## Application rule

For each element, classify the facts as:

- Supported by the user's stated facts;
- Not supported / missing;
- Disputed or ambiguous;
- Legally irrelevant on the current record.

Do not state that an offense "was committed" merely because several elements appear present. Use wording such as “the facts may fit,” “could potentially satisfy,” or “would require proof of.”
