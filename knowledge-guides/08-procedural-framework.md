# Procedural Framework

Use the current Code of Criminal Procedure and applicable special legislation for procedural questions.

## Main procedural topics

- Type of criminal action: public, public at private instance, or private.
- Standing of victim, complainant, civil actor, defendant, and third-party civil defendant.
- Jurisdiction and territorial/material competence.
- Investigation authority and role of the Ministerio Público and Policía Nacional.
- Rights of the accused from the first act of the proceeding.
- Arrest, detention, summons, rebellion, and habeas corpus.
- Searches, seizures, interception, bodily examinations, and other rights-restricting measures.
- Legality, preservation, production, exclusion, and evaluation of evidence.
- Coercive measures and review of those measures.
- Alternative resolutions: opportunity, conciliation, conditional suspension, abbreviated proceedings, when legally available.
- Duration of investigation and proceeding.
- Prescription, interruption, suspension, and extinction of the action.
- Preliminary hearing.
- Trial.
- Appeals and review.
- Execution of sentence and security measures.

## Rights checklist

When relevant, verify:
- presumption of innocence;
- right to remain silent and non-self-incrimination;
- right to counsel;
- right to know the accusation;
- right to communicate after arrest;
- right to interpreter;
- reasonable time;
- legality of evidence;
- judicial control of restrictions on liberty;
- victim participation and protection.

## Procedure/substance separation

Do not mix the elements of an offense with the procedural requirements for investigating or prosecuting it. A substantively punishable act may still raise procedural issues affecting evidence, jurisdiction, timeliness, or admissibility.

## Current-code rule

When an older statute cites a repealed Code of Criminal Procedure, research whether the current procedural code supplies the applicable mechanism. Do not reproduce obsolete procedural rules without clearly labeling them historical.
